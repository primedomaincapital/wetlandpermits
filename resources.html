Where wetland permitting actually happens.

Last checked: November 18, 2026

Wetland permitting sits at the crossing of three things: the Corps districts that issue the permits, the market that sells the credits to offset them, and the law that keeps redrawing the boundary of what counts as a wetland. The links below cover all three — the regulators and their programs, the data that sizes the market, the state programs that show how it works on the ground, and the court decision that changed the rules.

Regulators: the Corps, the EPA, and the permit

U.S. Army Corps of Engineers — Nationwide Permits Finalized, January 7, 2026

The Corps' announcement that it will reissue 56 existing nationwide permits and issue one new one, while dropping the finfish mariculture permit — the current state of the general-permit system.

Government Accountability Office — Clean Water Act: Costs of Compensatory Mitigation Activities, GAO-24-107493

The September 2024 report that puts the hard numbers on the permit system: more than 73,000 acres covered by Corps permits in fiscal year 2023, and credit prices from under $100,000 to over $3 million.

Government Accountability Office — Corps Oversight of Compensatory Mitigation, GAO-26-106827

The April 2026 follow-up, based on 85 permit files from three districts, finding the Corps has improved its oversight of mitigation since 2005 but still needs implementation guidance.

U.S. Environmental Protection Agency — Section 401 Water Quality Certification for the 2021 Nationwide Permits

A full example of the second gate in the process: the state and tribal water-quality certification that must accompany a federal permit before it is valid.

Law: Sackett and the waters of the United States

FedLaws — Sackett v. EPA: New Wetlands Test and Federal Jurisdiction Limits

A clear read of the May 25, 2023 decision: the unanimous judgment, the 5–4 majority's continuous surface connection test, and the end of the significant nexus standard.

Legal 500 — EPA and Army Corps Release Proposed WOTUS Rule After Sackett

The November 17, 2025 proposed rule explained: how it would define relatively permanent waters and continuous surface connection, and why the country is split between two regulatory regimes.

Sidley Austin — EPA and Army Corps Seek Comment on Another WOTUS Alternative

The September 4, 2026 supplemental notice, which would narrow the definition further to perennial water and remove "adjacent" and "abutting" from the rules altogether.

U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service — 2019 Wetlands Status and Trends Report

The congressionally mandated decadal report behind the loss figures: 116.4 million acres of wetlands in 2019, a net loss of 221,000 acres since 2009, and vegetated wetlands down 670,000 acres.

Market: the banks, the credits, and the prices

MitiVision — What Is a Mitigation Bank?

The plainest entry point to the market, built on the federal RIBITS registry: 5,151 bank and in-lieu fee sites, 3,344 of them mitigation banks, and only 2,195 approved with credits available.

MitiVision — In-Lieu Fee Mitigation Rates by State

The only officially published prices in the market: a median wetland credit of $100,000 across 110 published rates, with the warning that a list price is not a bank's transaction price.

MitiVision — Mitigation Banking Companies

The operator directory drawn from RIBITS: 67 companies running 1,427 banks, led by Resource Environmental Solutions with 215 banks across 20 states.

University of Florida Warrington College of Business — Banking on Mitigation

The best single narrative of the Florida market: 131 banks on 227,000 acres, credits running short in the fastest-growing counties, and the bankers building the new supply.

LegalClarity — How Much Is an Acre of Wetland Worth?

A valuation firm's walk through what drives wetland and credit prices, from the GAO's rural-to-coastal range to Florida's basin-by-basin credit costs.

States: how mitigation works on the ground

Wisconsin DNR — Status of Wisconsin's Wetland Compensatory Mitigation Program, 2023–2024

A state biennial report with the arithmetic laid bare: 116 permits needing more than 163 credits, 11 new banks adding 1,438 acres, and 5,225 acres restored since 2012.

Snyder & Associates — Mitigation Banking: Offsetting Unavoidable Impacts to Aquatic Resources

A practitioner's guide to how a bank works and what it costs, with Iowa credit prices — emergent wetlands $35,000 to $75,000, forested $45,000 to $105,000.

West Virginia DEP — In Lieu Fee Stream and Wetland Mitigation

A state in-lieu fee program's own fee schedule: $127,500 per wetland credit and $800 per stream credit, set by the state's valuation metric.

The Climate Watch — U.S. Commits $4.4 Million to Agricultural Wetland Offset Projects

The November 2026 USDA announcement of funding for seven new wetland mitigation banking projects, and the 41 projects the program has backed since 2016.
